# Source notes and corrections

- Printed p2 (PDF16), vector-space axiom list: adaptation explicitly includes scalar identity 1|V⟩=|V⟩, needed in the standard definition.
- Printed p13 (PDF27), text following (1.3.9): complex ket-to-bra correspondence is antilinear; Chinese adaptation states this accurately.
- Printed p16 (PDF30), orthogonal-independence statement: adaptation explicitly requires nonzero vectors.
- Printed pp65–70: vanishing boundary form establishes symmetry on a chosen domain; self-adjointness additionally requires equality of operator/adjoint domains. Adaptation distinguishes these, and solves fixed-end modes using the self-adjoint second derivative rather than asserting a Dirichlet first derivative is self-adjoint.
- Scanned PDF omits the blank printed page74: printedp73 is PDF87, next PDF88 is printedp75. Chapter1 content ends at PDF87.
- Scanned PDF also omits blank printed page114: printedp113 is PDF126; PDF127 is printedp115 and begins Chapter4. Chapter3 content is PDF120–126.
- Printed p102 (PDF115), discrete parity discussion: the parity symmetry subgroup has no nontrivial infinitesimal symmetry. The map (q,p)→(−q,−p) can nevertheless be embedded in a continuous canonical flow; intermediate maps need not preserve a general Hamiltonian. The adaptation distinguishes a continuous symmetry from merely a continuous canonical embedding.
- Printed p140 (PDF152), Figure4.3 caption calls λ a frequency, while the figure and surrounding text use λ as wavelength. The adaptation consistently calls λ the wavelength and distinguishes this microscope estimate from the standard-deviation uncertainty relation.
- Printed p164 (PDF176), finite-well odd-state threshold: the exercise's necessary-condition wording includes equality. The adaptation distinguishes the strict condition for a normalizable odd bound state from the threshold κ=0, whose exterior tail does not decay.
- Printed p174 (PDF186), (5.4.28): the scan contains absolute-value bars around E₀−V₀. For E₀<V₀ the adaptation writes the equivalent positive expression V₀−E₀; this is a simplification, not a source correction.
- Printed pp176–178 (PDF188–190), Theorems15–16: the adaptation spells out regularity, connected domain and boundary assumptions, extends the Wronskian proof through nodes using initial-value uniqueness, and distinguishes a real eigenbasis from an arbitrary complex superposition.
- Printed p182 (PDF194), Gaussian (6.9): for the printed exponent and normalization, the exact standard deviations are Δ/√2 and ℏ/(√2Δ). The surrounding source prose uses Δ and ℏ/Δ as width scales; the adaptation distinguishes the parameter from the exact standard deviations.
- Printed p183 (PDF195), Taylor-force discussion: approximation errors are compared with an explicit force scale, keeping units consistent. Free spreading is used as the stated estimate, without extending inevitable growth to arbitrary confining potentials.
- Printed p188 (PDF200), (7.2.5): the positive square root gives the speed or the right-moving branch. The adaptation uses the speed magnitude when deriving the time-weighted classical position density.
- Printed p195 (PDF207), (7.3.27): the explicit Hermite factors omit the dimensionless coordinate scaling used in (7.3.22). The adaptation writes the equivalent normalized-eigenfunction spectral sum, with the correct physical-coordinate dependence.
- Printed p216 (PDF228), radial momentum discussion: the integration-by-parts expression is treated as formal symmetry on an appropriate domain. Self-adjointness on a half-line, boundary conditions and the Hamiltonian domain require separate analysis; the quantum correction in (7.4.44) retains its minus sign.
- The scan omits blank printed page222: printed221 is PDF233, and PDF234 is printed223 and begins Chapter8.

- Printed p232 (PDF243), (8.6.10): the source prints one half of L_vv as m; differentiating the displayed Lagrangian gives m/2. The adaptation writes m/2, consistent with (8.6.11). The d coefficient multiplies velocity to the first power, not velocity squared.
